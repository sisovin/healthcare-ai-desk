## 1. C-01 — M2.5 crash recovery after `CONSUMED`

### 1.1 Meaning of consumption

`CONSUMED` means that M2.5 atomically recorded a single authorized dispatch
attempt for the exact immutable payload/destination binding. It does **not**
mean that a provider received, accepted, processed, retained, or successfully
completed the request. It cannot be reset or reused as a new authorization.

M2.5 owns the attempt record, the atomic reservation/consumption transition,
and all recovery/outcome transitions. M4 may request a decision and report a
sanitized outcome observation; it cannot consume, reset, adjudicate, or reuse
an attempt.

### 1.2 Durable recovery state and reconciliation rule

For a consumed attempt, M2.5 separately records a provider-outcome state:

```text
NO_OUTCOME_EVIDENCE
PROVIDER_CONFIRMED_ACCEPTED
PROVIDER_CONFIRMED_REJECTED
UNCERTAIN
```

On startup and on a bounded recovery schedule, an M2.5-owned reconciler finds
attempts in `CONSUMED` with `NO_OUTCOME_EVIDENCE` whose sender lease/expected
completion window has expired. It atomically changes their outcome state to
`UNCERTAIN`, records minimal recovery provenance, and blocks automatic resend.

The same fail-closed reconciliation applies when a sender crashes:

- after `CONSUMED` but before socket write;
- during socket write;
- after provider receipt but before acknowledgement persistence; or
- after acknowledgement but before local outcome persistence.

An authorized, independently verifiable provider receipt/webhook may append
`PROVIDER_CONFIRMED_ACCEPTED` or `PROVIDER_CONFIRMED_REJECTED`; it cannot alter
the original payload/destination binding or turn an old decision into a new
send authorization. If no trustworthy outcome evidence exists, `UNCERTAIN`
remains the safe disposition.

### 1.3 Retry and operator rule

An `UNCERTAIN` or consumed attempt must never be resent with its original
decision. Any proposed retry requires a fresh exact-payload M2.5 decision, a
new `egress_attempt_id`, and whatever operational/human approval the governing
policy requires. If the provider supports idempotency, an approved sender may
use a stable logical-operation key under the new attempt; this reduces provider
duplication risk but does not create exactly-once delivery semantics.

Without a provider idempotency mechanism, automatic retry after `UNCERTAIN` is
prohibited. M2.5/operations must retain a decision record explaining whether a
manual retry, abandonment, or other approved workflow is permitted.

### 1.4 Required future evidence

M4-T06 must include synthetic crash/recovery cases at `NOT_SENT`, `RESERVED`,
`CONSUMED-before-send`, during send, after send-before-ack, and after
provider-ack-before-local-persistence. Every scenario must show no reuse of the
old decision and no claim that `CONSUMED` proves provider success.

## 2. C-02 — Staged receipt and Qdrant physical readiness

### 2.1 Receipt states

M4.3 must distinguish a logical receipt from verified physical point readiness:

```text
PENDING_WRITE -> WRITE_ACKNOWLEDGED -> PHYSICALLY_VERIFIED
                                         -> MISSING | MISMATCHED | UNAVAILABLE
```

Only `PHYSICALLY_VERIFIED` may contribute to a `STAGED_READY` generation. A
Qdrant upsert acknowledgement alone is not verification.

### 2.2 Verified receipt boundary

For each deterministic point, the future indexer must:

1. upsert using the approved Qdrant write-consistency/profile for the chosen
   deployment;
2. perform a read-after-write at the documented read-consistency/profile using
   the deterministic point ID; and
3. compare the returned immutable payload identifiers and a non-sensitive
   expected payload/vector representation hash against the authoritative
   receipt candidate.

Only after successful readback may the worker commit the MySQL receipt as
`PHYSICALLY_VERIFIED`. The receipt must retain Qdrant collection/schema
generation, point ID, consistency profile identifier, expected payload hash,
verification timestamp, and minimal request/correlation reference—never raw
chunk text, vectors, credentials, or patient identifiers.

The exact Qdrant API/version, write/read consistency semantics, and durability
claim must be recorded in governance before M4.3 implementation. A remote
vector service, replica, snapshot, backup, telemetry, or support boundary
remains subject to M2.5/classification controls.

### 2.3 Missing-point and rebuild fencing

If a MySQL receipt exists but authoritative verification cannot find the
physical point, finds a different immutable payload, or cannot establish the
required consistency:

- mark that receipt `MISSING`, `MISMATCHED`, or `UNAVAILABLE` with a sanitized
  error code;
- prevent the affected staged generation from becoming `STAGED_READY` or
  active;
- for an already active generation, mark its index health `DEGRADED`, trigger
  an auditable re-verification/reindex intent, and preserve MySQL access and
  revocation fences; and
- never fabricate a physical-ready receipt from an old MySQL record or snapshot.

Snapshot restore and rebuild require new physical verification of every expected
point before generation activation. Qdrant loss can reduce retrieval
availability, but it must not weaken tenant, ACL, revocation, source-provenance,
or egress controls.

### 2.4 Required future evidence

M4-T04/M4-T05/M4-T09 must include receipt-then-point-loss, mismatched point,
readback outage, snapshot/restore, partial rebuild, duplicate upsert, and
staged-generation activation denial when any required point is not physically
verified.

## 3. Governance readiness versus decision lifecycle

The governance register uses two distinct values:

- **Readiness:** `PENDING`, `BLOCKED`, `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, or `VERIFIED` is an
  overview/entry-gate indicator.
- **Decision lifecycle:** `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `EXPIRED`,
  `SUPERSEDED`, or `REJECTED` describes an individual decision record.

`readiness: PENDING` with `status: DRAFT` is the required initial state. Only
an in-scope, current record with `status: APPROVED` plus independent evidence
can support `readiness: VERIFIED`. Neither value grants infrastructure or M4.2
execution authority by itself.

## 4. Decision

Revision 4 closes C-01, C-02, and the governance-status documentation drift at
the **design-contract level**. This is not an operational certification or
evidence that M2.5, Qdrant, or any worker has passed integration testing.
Governance-record collection may continue, but M4.2 schema planning remains
blocked until all seven decision records are actually approved and independently
verified, including compatibility with the real MySQL/Alembic, M2.5, and
Qdrant interfaces. M3.2.1 live gates remain separate and pending.
